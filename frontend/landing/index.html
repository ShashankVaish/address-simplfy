The problem
An Indian address is directions for a human.

      
Large parts of India have no systematic street numbering, so people write landmarks. Every last-mile system that fights this loses three times.

    

    

      
Cost 1
The rider phones the customer
On almost every order. Before leaving, on the way, and again at the gate.

      
Cost 2
The first attempt fails
Fuel, rider time, a second dispatch — and a customer who is now annoyed.

      
Cost 3
The knowledge evaporates
"Gate 3, ask the guard, lift on the left" dies in the rider's head. The next rider starts from zero.

    

    
"h no 14 behind shiv mandir near gupta general store opp water tank ramesh nagar delhi 110015 call before coming" ← a real one. Landmark-based addressing isn't laziness; the system has to work with it.

What's different
Not an address cleaner.

      
Five decisions that separate a resolution engine from a regex with a model bolted on.

DIGIPIN is the canonical output39JJTT4565 · ~4 m cellIndia Post's national geocode, computed locally from the coordinate with the vendored official encoder. No API, no cost, works offline on a rider's phone.Ten differently-worded addresses that land in the same cell are provably the same doorstep — a free deduplication key.

        
The landmark graph learnsEventBridge → learnerA confirmed delivery bumps each landmark's observation count, nudges its coordinate toward the rider's tap (damped by how often it's been seen), and learns the customer's spelling as an alias.Measured, not asserted: one confirmed delivery takes a locality from 6% to 81% graph-geocoded.

Decision

          
DENY

          
no permit matched: outside contact hours (local time 23:00; allowed 09:00–20:00)

          

        

        
// A forbid beats every permit. No principal named: nobody overrides an opt-out.
@id("contact-opted-out")
forbid(principal, action == Action::"contactCustomer", resource in OrderGroup::"opted_out");

@id("contact-allowed-window")
permit(principal == Agent::"clarifier", action == Action::"contactCustomer", resource in OrderGroup::"pending_resolution")
when { context.confidence_pct < 80 && context.messages_sent_for_order == 0 && context.local_hour >= 9 && context.local_hour <= 20 && context.channel == "sms" };

Where it fits
Anywhere a rider is about to make a call.

      
One API, one contract. The live console is for the operations team; the resolver is for the platform.

    

    

      
Quick commerce
10-minute delivery
The order is placed once and delivered dozens of times. The second delivery to "behind Shiv Mandir" should not need the phone.

      
E-commerce
Returns and repeat orders
The same doorstep, written five ways over a year, resolves to one DIGIPIN cell — one record, not five.

      
Logistics
Address validation at checkout
NEEDS_INFO with a single targeted question at order time is cheaper than a failed attempt tomorrow.

      
Operations
A human queue with reasons
Every case that needs a person arrives with the evidence and the policy that sent it — never a silent drop.
